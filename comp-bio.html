TWF2 Knockdown and Reduced α-Synuclein Expression

A Screen of 300 CRISPRi Perturbations in Human Embryonic Stem Cells

I screened all 300 CRISPRi perturbations in 491,046 single cells for knockdowns that lower SNCA, the gene behind α-synuclein, while leaving the rest of the transcriptome alone. TWF2 (Twinfilin-2) was the only one to meet both prespecified criteria: 92.4% lower SNCA with just 14 genes altered.

The Question

Parkinson's disease is the second most common neurodegenerative disorder. Its hallmark is the buildup of α-synuclein, encoded by SNCA, into Lewy bodies, and α-synuclein dosage is genetically tied to disease: duplication or triplication of the SNCA locus is enough to cause Parkinson's. Lowering SNCA is therefore an actively pursued therapeutic strategy.

But a gene whose knockdown lowers SNCA is only a useful starting point if it does so without reorganizing the rest of the transcriptome. Most strong SNCA reducers are chromatin regulators that change thousands of genes. So I screened every perturbation on two axes at once: how much it lowers SNCA, and how many other genes it changes.

Key Finding

TWF2 was the only perturbation of 300 to meet both prespecified criteria

TWF2 (Twinfilin-2) knockdown was associated with 92.4% lower SNCA mRNA while altering just 14 genes. The other strong SNCA reducers, EP300 and DOT1L, each altered more than 2,000.

The criteria were fixed before the data were loaded: a perturbation qualifies if it reduces SNCA by at least 50% (adjusted p < 0.10) and alters no more than 20 genes. Twenty-eight perturbations significantly changed SNCA; twelve reduced it, four by at least 50%, and only TWF2 also stayed under the gene-count limit.

Held in Every Testable Batch

Re-estimating the effect separately within each processing batch, SNCA was lower in 30 of 30 batches with at least 20 TWF2 cells (sign test p = 1.9 × 10⁻⁹; median reduction 94.8%, range 77.0 to 100%).

Why TWF2 Stands Out

A screen that measured SNCA alone would have flagged EP300 and DOT1L as hits too. Measuring each perturbation's transcriptome-wide footprint alongside its effect on SNCA is what separates them from TWF2:

Computational Methods

1

Dataset

Arc Institute Virtual Cell Challenge 2025: CRISPRi knockdown in H1 human embryonic stem cells, profiled with 10x Genomics Flex chemistry. I combined the training, validation and test releases (all 48 processing batches appear in all three) for 491,046 cells, including 114,528 non-targeting controls, across 300 target genes.

2

Preprocessing

Pseudobulk profiles were built from raw counts before normalization. Cells were then library-size normalized and log-transformed in scanpy, and genes detected in more than 5% of control cells were kept (12,760 of 18,080).

3

Differential Expression

Each perturbation was compared to batch-matched non-targeting controls with a tie-corrected Wilcoxon rank-sum test and Benjamini-Hochberg correction across all 12,760 genes. The tie correction matters: single-cell counts are heavily tied at zero, and omitting it inflates significance. The engine was checked against scipy.stats.mannwhitneyu and scanpy. Thresholds (adjusted p < 0.1, |log2FC| > 0.4) follow Replogle et al.

4

Screen & Replicate-Level Analysis

All 300 perturbations were screened against the prespecified criteria. The shortlist was re-tested with batch-paired pseudobulk comparisons and DESeq2, and the TWF2 effect was re-estimated independently within each batch.

5

Infrastructure

The full pipeline runs as a single Python notebook (scanpy, SciPy, PyDESeq2, PyTorch) on an NVIDIA A100 GPU in about 15 minutes, with a fixed random seed. Code is on GitHub.

Robustness Checks

Several checks test whether the result depends on analysis choices:

✓

Null Calibration

Splitting the controls in half and comparing them to each other yields essentially zero altered genes, so the gene counts sit above an empirical floor

✓

Control Subsampling

Across five seeded subsamples of 1,000 controls, TWF2 altered 10.6 ± 1.1 genes and SNCA reduction stayed at 92.3% ± 0.2%

✓

Threshold Sensitivity

The gap between TWF2 and direct SNCA knockdown held across a grid of FDR and fold-change cutoffs (3.8× to 11.8×)

✓

Knockdown Efficiency

TWF2 itself was knocked down by 96.7%, in line with the 95.4% median across all 300 targets

✓

Leave-One-Batch-Out

Dropping each batch in turn does not change the result, so no single batch drives it

✓

Chromosomal Separation

TWF2 (3p21.2) and SNCA (4q22.1) are on different chromosomes, and CRISPRi doesn't cut DNA, ruling out a cis-genomic artifact

Context & Limitations

TWF2 encodes twinfilin-2, an actin-monomer-binding protein. α-synuclein binds actin, and actin regulators such as cofilin and gelsolin protect against α-synuclein toxicity in animal models, so a link is biologically plausible. Still, transcriptional, post-transcriptional and indirect cytoskeletal-signaling explanations all fit these data, and no mechanism is proposed.

Association, Not Causation

These are transcript-level associations in a stem cell line. They flag TWF2 as a candidate for experiments, not a validated target.

Batches Aren't Biological Replicates

The 48 batches are processing units within one experiment. Blocking on batch removes pseudoreplication but doesn't demonstrate independent replication.

No Guide-Level Check

Each cell received a jointly delivered guide pair, so per-guide concordance can't be tested for TWF2 or SNCA in this dataset.

Gene Count Isn't Specificity

The number of altered genes depends on thresholds, control set size and toxicity, which is why sensitivity and matched-sample analyses are reported with it.

Next Steps

Test TWF2 knockdown in iPSC-derived dopaminergic neurons with independent guides or siRNA, then measure α-synuclein protein and aggregation, not just mRNA. TWF2 knockout mice are viable, and a small molecule (salvianolic acid E) is already known to disrupt the TWF2-YAP interaction, which would make follow-up work more tractable.
